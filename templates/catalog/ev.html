Under Net Energy Metering an exported kWh is credited what an imported one costs in the same window, so charging midday on solar costs about the same as charging overnight: either way it's an off-peak kWh. What matters is staying out of 4–9pm, which is what "Saved $" measures. Tariff details.

A session is a contiguous run of charging power > 0 (collectors/tesla.py); it breaks on an explicit "not charging" reading or a coverage gap, never on raw elapsed time. Actual $ prices each sample at the TOU rate in effect when it happened; shifted $ re-prices the same energy at that date's off-peak rate — what charging entirely outside 4–9pm would have cost. Solar-covered charging is priced the same as grid charging: under NEM it's the export credit given up, which equals the import rate.
