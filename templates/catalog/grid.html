Tariff: E-TOU-C under Net Energy Metering

PG&E rate schedule E-TOU-C, "Time-of-Use (Peak Pricing 4 - 9 p.m. Every Day)". Two periods only: peak 4–9pm every day, off-peak every other hour. Summer is June–September, winter October–May. Generation comes from WestLight Energy (the community choice provider, formerly PCE); PG&E does delivery. True-up is in April.

Solar is on Net Energy Metering (NEM), PG&E's original net-metering program, not the newer Net Billing Tariff ("NEM 3" / Solar Billing Plan). Under NEM, exports net against imports kWh-for-kWh within each TOU period: an exported kWh is credited what an imported kWh costs in the same window. Practical upshot:

  • Midday on solar and overnight off-peak cost about the same. A kWh the house or car uses at noon is a kWh not exported at the off-peak credit; at 11pm it's imported at that same rate.
  • The lever is avoiding 4–9pm: using power then costs the peak rate, or forfeits a peak-rate export credit.
  • Exception: if a true-up year ends net-exporting, the surplus is paid only a few cents per kWh, so extra off-peak use then is nearly free.

The bill gives the generation credit and PCIA only as dollar totals, so they're spread evenly over the period's 171 net kWh. WestLight raised its rate mid-cycle; pricing each of its lines at its own rate, these reproduce the bill to the cent. The baseline credit counts every summer kWh because summer months net-export, keeping net usage under the allowance. The $24/month Base Services Charge (since March 2026) is fixed and not per kWh.
